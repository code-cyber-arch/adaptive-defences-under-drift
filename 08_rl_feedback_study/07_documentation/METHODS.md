# Integrated methods wording

Experiment 2 compares learned reset policies and same-information controls across SEA, Random RBF and RADAR using ADWIN, HDDM-W, Hellinger and the D3-inspired monitor. Phase 04 trains separate two-state and four-state controllers for every dataset and detector. Five RL seeds per combination produce 120 controllers. Training budgets, reward definitions and episode order are matched across detectors within each dataset. Q-tables are frozen before evaluation, and models are evaluated only with their training dataset and detector.

SEA and RBF use separate generated realizations for training, validation and evaluation, with five evaluation seeds per dataset. RADAR evaluation uses five poisoning assignments on its fixed evaluation period and one shared clean condition. RL seeds are 7, 17, 27, 37 and 47 for every dataset, detector and state representation. RADAR uses chronological periods: the first 242,000 observations for fitting, rows 243,000–338,999 for validation and rows 340,000–484,752 for evaluation. One thousand observations separate adjacent periods. Each period has its own warm-up and independently generated poisoning conditions. Replay sources and generated replacement features are restricted to the relevant period, preventing evaluation observations from entering training through attack construction.

Within each dataset and detector, learned controllers are compared with screened continuous learning, the fixed screened response, a rule using the same error information and a validation-selected deterministic mapping. All sixteen mappings of the four states to two actions are considered separately for each dataset and detector. Evaluation outcomes do not select a detector, controller seed or mapping. The selected mappings and learned policies share the same input and screening rules, while the existing buffered refit remains part of confirmed reset.

Feedback sensitivity covers all seven conditions, all three datasets and all four detectors, including both RL representations with five fitting seeds each. A common five-percent reservation is maintained while nested usable protected samples of one, two or five percent are released after one or five blocks. Unreleased protected observations remain excluded from training and scoring. All controllers remain frozen, and missing evidence causes the gate to reject a candidate. Prediction, exposure, legitimate withholding and reset activity are measured together; RADAR also reports malicious recall and benign false-positive rate.

The existing RQ1 and RQ2 remain unchanged. Repeated no-reset controls, RL seeds and attack assignments on one RADAR stream are not treated as independent datasets. Grand means weight poisoning conditions equally. Chronological policy splits preserve the supplied feature vectors and do not remove their known provenance limitations.

The complete baseline, passive-detector, fixed-response and filtering design is specified in [FULL_STUDY.md](../docs/FULL_STUDY.md). All three experiments use the same evaluation partitions.
